Decorative flake floors that hide the sand

Sand, water spots and tire marks have nowhere to show on a flake floor. The multi-color speckle hides it all, adds grip and looks sharp for years.

In a town where sand blows under every garage door and rides home in every boat, a plain, solid-color floor shows everything. That is why flake floors are such a natural fit for Havasu. Their multi-colored speckle breaks up the surface so fine grit, water spots and the occasional tire mark simply blend in.

They are also a lot more customizable than people think. Chip size, color blend and how densely the chips are spread all change the look, from bold and textured to subtle and refined. This page walks through how flake floors are built and how to choose one you will love.

Why flake and sand get along

A solid-color floor reads as one smooth plane, so anything on top of it stands out. A flake floor is busy by design. Every square foot already has a mix of light, mid and dark specks, so a few grains of sand or a dried water spot just becomes part of the pattern.

That buys you time between cleanings, which is what most people actually want from a garage or laundry room floor. It also makes flake floors more forgiving of the slab underneath. Old crack repairs and patch lines that would show through a solid color disappear under a full broadcast.

Add the natural texture the chips create, and you get a floor with better grip when wet. For garages where boats get rinsed, or laundry rooms off the pool, that matters.

Blends inspired by the lake and desert

There are dozens of stock blends. These are the color families that tend to look best in Havasu homes.

Shoreline

Soft grey, white and a hint of aqua. Clean and bright, with a coastal feel that suits modern homes.

Beach sand

Tan, cream and light brown. Blends perfectly with the sand it is trying to hide.

Desert granite

Grey, tan and black. The all-rounder, hiding sand, tire marks and water spots equally well.

Sunset

Warm terracotta, tan and charcoal, inspired by evening light on the mountains across the lake.

The base coat color matters too. We tint it to match your blend so the floor looks consistent, even where the chip coverage is lighter.

Full broadcast or partial broadcast?

Full broadcast means chips are thrown until the base coat is completely covered. It gives the most texture, the best camouflage and the classic granite look. For garages, RV bays and shops, it is usually the best choice.

Partial broadcast leaves some of the base color showing between chips. It is cleaner and more modern-looking, and works well in laundry rooms, studios and garages where you want a lighter touch. It shows a bit more grit than full broadcast, but much less than a plain solid floor.

Chip size changes things too. Quarter-inch chips are bold and textured. Eighth-inch chips give a finer speckle. Micro chips read almost like a stone finish from standing height.

How we install a flake floor

  1. Grind and repair

    Diamond grinding opens the concrete, then cracks and pits are filled.

  2. Tinted base coat

    Applied in sections sized to the temperature of the day.

  3. Hand broadcast

    Chips thrown in overlapping passes for even coverage.

  4. Scrape and clean

    Raised and loose chips are removed once the base cures.

  5. Clear topcoat

    A UV-stable topcoat seals the chips, with texture added as standard.

Flake floors in Havasu heat

Heat changes how a flake floor goes down. On a warm slab, the base coat thickens quickly, and chips have to be broadcast while it is still wet enough to grab them. We work in smaller sections and start early so every part of the floor gets the same coverage.

Sun is the other factor. Clear topcoats and some chip colors respond differently to UV. For garages with west-facing doors and covered patios that catch the afternoon sun, we use UV-stable topcoats and point you toward chip colors that hold up best.

And because sand is part of daily life here, we make sure the topcoat is thick enough to stand up to grit without wearing through.

What makes a flake floor look professional

Even coverage, a properly smooth topcoat and clean edges are what separate a great flake floor from a patchy one. Uneven broadcasting leaves light spots and clumps that cannot be fixed later. A thin topcoat leaves the surface rough enough to trap dirt. Rushed edges at thresholds and stem walls look sloppy every time the door opens. These are details that take experience, especially in the heat.

Caring for your flake floor

Sweep or blow out sand regularly. Wash with warm water, a neutral cleaner and a soft brush or mop. Avoid citrus degreasers and harsh solvents. If the topcoat dulls in traffic lanes after many years, it can be scuffed and recoated without touching the chips.

What a flake floor costs

Price depends mostly on size and slab condition. Removing old coatings, repairing cracks and dealing with oil-stained areas add prep time. Full broadcast uses more chips than partial broadcast, and custom blends cost more than stock options. Coating stem walls and extra texture are small add-ons. We list every part in your written quote.

Flake, solid or metallic?

If you are torn between finishes, think about how the space is used. Flake is the practical pick for anywhere that sees sand, water, tools or tires; it hides the most and forgives the most. Solid colors look clean and modern but show dust and marks sooner, so they suit gyms, studios and spaces that get cleaned often. Metallic epoxy is the showpiece, best indoors or in a garage that is more display than workshop.

Plenty of homes mix them: flake in the garage, micro flake in the laundry room and a metallic finish in the game room.

Matching the floor to your home

A garage floor is part of the view every time the door goes up, so it is worth matching it to the house. Warm stucco and tile roofs pair naturally with beach sand and sunset blends. Modern homes with white or grey exteriors look sharp with shoreline or desert granite. Inside, look at cabinet colors and wall paint too. Holding a few chip samples against them during the quote visit is the easiest way to decide.

Flake floor questions

Which blend hides sand best?

Beach sand and desert granite blends are the best at hiding sand, since they share its tones. Very dark or very light blends show it more.

Is a flake floor rough on bare feet?

A good topcoat smooths the chip edges into a fine, even texture. It is comfortable to walk on and still grips when wet.

Can I see the colors before choosing?

Yes. We bring chip samples to every quote so you can see them on your actual floor in your own light.

Will the chips fade?

Indoors, chips sealed under a quality topcoat hold their color very well. On patios in full sun, we recommend UV-stable topcoats and colors that resist fading.

How long does it take?

A typical garage takes one to two days. Laundry rooms and small spaces can be faster.

Can a flake floor go outside?

On covered patios, yes, with an exterior topcoat. Uncovered pool decks usually do better with a cooler, textured system. See patio coatings.

Do flake floors add value to a home?

A finished garage tends to stand out in listing photos and walkthroughs, especially in a town where buyers care about boat and RV space. It is hard to put a number on it, but it is a common upgrade buyers notice.

Can you add flakes to just part of a floor?

Yes. Some people choose a solid border with a flake field, or different blends for different areas. We will help you plan a layout that looks intentional.
