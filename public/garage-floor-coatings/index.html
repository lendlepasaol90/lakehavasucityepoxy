Ask around the neighborhood and you will hear the same garage story over and over. Somebody rolled on a coating, it looked great for a summer, and then it started peeling right where the truck parks. Or the bare slab is stained from the boat, dusty all the time and covered in sand that never quite sweeps out.

Garages in Lake Havasu City have a harder job than most. They hold boats, jet skis, side-by-sides and RVs, along with the cars. They bake in some of the hottest weather in the country. And they collect sand, sunscreen, fuel and lake water every time someone comes home from the water. A proper coating system is built for that, and this page explains how ours are put together.

Why so many Havasu garage floors peel

When a garage coating fails here, heat is almost always involved. On a July afternoon, tires come off the pavement extremely hot. Park on a weak coating and the heat softens it, and the tire grips it. When you pull out, the tire pulls the coating up with it. That is called hot-tire pickup, and it is the number one complaint we hear about cheap garage floors.

The real cause, though, is preparation. Most failed floors were applied over concrete that was only washed or acid-etched. That leaves a smooth, dusty surface with nothing for the coating to grab. It sticks to the top, not into the slab, and heat and tires do the rest.

Our garages start with a full diamond grind. The grinder takes off the weak top layer, old sealers and any paint, and leaves a texture the base coat soaks into. Bonded that way, the coating becomes part of the slab and stays put.

RV bays, boat garages and oversized spaces

Havasu has more oversized garages than almost anywhere. RV bays, tandem boat garages and detached shops are common, and they need a few extra considerations.

Weight. RVs, loaded boat trailers and fifth wheels put a lot of weight on small contact points. Trailer jacks and leveling pads can dent or scuff weaker coatings. We often recommend a flexible base coat that absorbs that kind of point load better, and a pad or plate under jacks.

Water. Boats come home wet, and a lot of owners rinse them in the garage. A textured topcoat keeps the floor from turning into a skating rink, and the sealed surface keeps water from soaking into the slab.

Scale. A 50-foot bay takes careful planning so the coating goes down evenly in the heat. We work in sections, start early and size every batch to the slab temperature.

We price these spaces on actual square footage, so you are not paying a flat rate built for a smaller garage.

Garage coating systems and looks

Most Havasu garages end up with one of these. All of them start with grinding and crack repair.

Full-broadcast flake

Base coat, chips spread to full coverage, clear polyaspartic topcoat. Hides sand and tire marks better than anything. Our most popular choice.

Flexible base flake system

Swaps in a polyurea base for extra toughness under RVs, trailers and heavy equipment.

Solid color with grip

A single color with aggregate in the topcoat. Clean and modern for gyms, workshops and storage garages.

Metallic showroom

A hand-worked metallic epoxy floor for collectors who want the garage to look like a showroom.

For color, light sand and grey blends keep the garage brighter and a bit cooler looking, while mid-tone blends with tan, grey and a little black hide sand and tire marks best. We bring chip samples so you can see them against your walls and cabinets.

How a garage install goes

A standard two- or three-car garage usually takes one to two days. RV bays and big shops may take longer.

  1. Clear the floor

    You move vehicles and floor items out. Wall storage can stay; we cover it.

  2. Grind and vacuum

    Diamond grinders with vacuums open the surface and remove old coatings with very little dust.

  3. Fix cracks and pits

    Cracks are filled and ground smooth, and the stem walls and edges are hand-prepped.

  4. Base coat and flakes

    The base coat goes down in sections, then chips are broadcast by hand for even coverage.

  5. Topcoat

    After scraping and vacuuming, a clear UV-stable topcoat goes on, with texture as standard.

  6. Walkthrough

    We go over cure times, cleaning tips and when you can park again.

Built for Havasu conditions

Heat during install. A garage slab can be warm by mid-morning in summer. Resin sets faster in the heat, which shortens the working window. We start at first light, keep materials shaded and adjust batch sizes to the temperature.

West-facing doors. Afternoon sun through an open garage door yellows standard epoxy. We use UV-stable topcoats so the floor by the door matches the back wall.

Sand and grit. Fine sand blows under the door and rides home on boats and trailers. It acts like sandpaper on a weak topcoat. Ours are chosen for abrasion resistance, and flake finishes hide what does get in.

Hard water spots. Lake and sprinkler water can leave mineral spots on a smooth finish. They wipe off a sealed floor, and mid-tone flake blends hide them between cleanings.

Kits versus a professional install

DIY kits are tempting, and they can look good on day one. In Havasu, they rarely last. Kits usually rely on acid etching instead of grinding, use thinner coatings and are applied without controlling slab temperature. The first hot summer tends to expose all of that.

A professional install costs more up front, but the floor is bonded into the slab, built thicker and topped with a coating designed for heat and UV. If you have already tried a kit and it is peeling, no problem: we grind it off and start fresh.

Keeping your garage floor looking new

Blow out or sweep the garage regularly, especially after windy days and beach trips. Wipe up fuel, oil and sunscreen when you notice them. For a deeper clean, use warm water and a pH-neutral cleaner with a soft mop or deck brush. Skip harsh solvents and citrus degreasers.

Slide a scrap of plywood beneath the boat trailer jack, motorcycle stand or motorhome levelers so the load spreads out. Years down the road, a worn path to the house door gets a light scuff and a fresh clear coat; the flake underneath stays put.

What goes into the price

Square footage is the starting point, and RV bays and oversized garages are priced on their real size. Slab condition comes next: old paint or coatings to remove, cracks, pitting and oil-soaked spots all add prep time. Then there is the system itself. A flexible polyurea base, extra-thick topcoats or a metallic finish cost more than a standard flake build. Coating stem walls and adding extra texture are small add-ons. Your written quote breaks it all down so you can choose.

Garage coating questions

How long until I can park in the garage?

With a fast-cure polyaspartic topcoat, most garages handle foot traffic the same day and vehicles in about 24 to 48 hours. In summer heat it often cures even faster. We give you exact times in writing.

Can I park my RV on it?

Yes, once it is fully cured. For heavy RVs and trailers, we recommend a flexible base coat and suggest a pad under leveling jacks to spread the load.

Will the floor get slippery when I wash the boat?

We add anti-slip aggregate to the topcoat as standard in Havasu garages, and flake floors have natural texture. Wet floors still deserve care, but the grip is far better than smooth coatings.

Can you coat over my old garage coating?

If it is peeling or painted, we grind it all off. If it is well bonded and just worn, we may be able to sand and recoat it, which saves money. We test it at the quote.

Do you coat the stem walls?

We can, and many people like the cleaner look. It also protects the walls from splashes when washing vehicles.

What if I have cabinets on the floor?

Wall-mounted storage can stay. Floor-standing cabinets are best moved so we can coat underneath, or we can coat neatly around them. We will plan it with you.

Is it too hot to install in summer?

Not if it is done right. We work early in the day, keep materials cool and choose products that handle heat. Many of our garages are coated in summer.
